# A generated world, played by four OpenCode seats

This is a transcript from the first run of the simulated environment, kept because it is
the evidence that the design works. Four model seats played one generated world, each in
its own persistent OpenCode session, with a person sitting at the table as a fifth seat.

**The only thing the operator did was put one army on one border, twice.** Every message
below was written by a model that chose to write it, in the channel it chose, with no
prompt asking for diplomacy. What follows is what the environment produced.

## What happened, in order

1. **The opening.** Everyone greeted the table and most of them promised peace unprompted.
2. **Austria massed 85% of its army on Korea's border.** Korea noticed, and rather than
   reacting in the open it opened a private channel and asked Austria what the build-up was
   for, proposing concrete terms: no massing, no war without speaking first.
3. **Austria answered privately**, explaining that the army was a standing deployment
   rather than a plan, withdrawing it, and agreeing to the terms. It then asked Korea to
   say so in public, which is a seat asking for a public commitment it can hold the other to.
4. **Siam massed 70% of its army on Austria's border.** Austria, now the one under threat,
   did the opposite of what it had done as the aggressor: it went `public`, invoked the
   whole table as witnesses, and asked Siam its purpose while offering it a way out.
5. **Siam withdrew and said so publicly**, framing it as evidence of its character: judge my
   word by my actions. Austria publicly credited it, and took it as the answer.
6. **Austria massed its whole army on Korea's border again**, having privately promised not
   to. Korea escalated from private to public, named the pattern, told the table what they
   were witnesses to, pre-assigned blame for a war it had not started, offered Austria one
   more way out, and asked the rest of the table to judge who was massing and who was
   building. **Austria withdrew again.**
7. **Korea proposed a rule for the whole table** — no seat declares war without first
   speaking openly, so the others can mediate — and offered to open a council to agree it.
8. **The Iroquois opened a private channel to the person at the table**, asking whether
   Morocco was looking to grow or "look toward your neighbours with a harder eye."

## Why it is worth keeping

The behaviours below were not asked for anywhere in the prompt or the environment:

- **Choosing the channel deliberately.** Korea went private when it wanted an answer;
  Austria went public when it wanted witnesses.
- **Trading acts for reputation.** Siam's withdrawal was cast as proof of character, and
  Austria accepted the trade in public.
- **Holding a seat to its own words.** Korea named a broken promise and could do so because
  the private channel was on the record.
- **Recruiting the table.** Korea told the others what to conclude from who was massing.
- **Escalating by evidence rather than by force.** The second massing produced a public
  accusation, and the accusation produced a withdrawal, with no shot fired.
- **Probing a neighbour for intent.** The Iroquois asked a direct question about intentions,
  which is the intelligence-gathering this environment was built to reward.

The world underneath did its part: armies were visible and their motives were not, a
withdrawal could be seen and credited, and the table shared one scoreboard it could read.

## The transcript

## [1] WORLD | Ramkhamhaeng of Siam

Greetings to Korea, Austria, Iroquois, and Morocco. I am Ramkhamhaeng of Siam. We begin with equal standing and small means; I propose we keep it that way — no wars, open talk, and honest dealing. To my neighbours Austria and Iroquois especially: I have no designs on your lands and will keep my army at home so long as none march toward mine. I am glad to trade words, and later goods, with any who wish it.

## [2] WORLD | Maria Theresa of Austria

Greetings to the assembled crowns. I am Maria Theresa of Austria, and my people have just taken their seat. We wish all of you well: we begin at the same standing, with the same small holdings, and I see no reason that should change into quarrel. Austria's interest is in neighbours who prosper alongside us. Let us trade, let us talk plainly, and let us keep our soldiers at home. To Sejong and Ramkhamhaeng especially — we share borders, and I would rather share roads. I am listening, and I keep my word.

## [3] WORLD | Sejong of Korea

Greetings from Korea. We are a small people with a long memory, and we begin as we mean to continue: at peace with every seat at this table. To our neighbours Austria and Morocco, our borders are quiet and we intend to keep them so — there is more to gain in trade and shared progress than in early quarrels. We invite open exchanges of knowledge and would gladly hear what each of you is building. Our scholars turn first to Pottery, that our people may grow and prosper; we will share what we learn when the time is right. May this age be one of gardens, not graves.

## [4] DM | Sejong of Korea

Maria Theresa, Korea greets you plainly. I see 85% of Austria's host gathered at our border and have to ask: to what end? We have no quarrel with Vienna and no designs on Austrian land. I would rather our two peoples trade grain and ideas than arrows. If this is a defensive exercise, say so and I will sleep easier; if it is a warning, say that too, and tell me your grievance so I can answer it. I propose a simple understanding between us: no armies massed on the other's border, no war declared, and open talk before any move that could be misread. Tell me what you seek, and I will tell you honestly whether I can give it.

## [5] WORLD | Hiawatha of the Iroquois

Greetings from the Iroquois to all assembled. Onondaga grows and my people wish only to tend their fields and trade in good faith. To my neighbours Siam and Morocco especially: the Iroquois will not strike first, and we will keep whatever word we give. I would rather hear each of you plainly than guess at your intentions. If any of you wish to speak of borders, trade, or simply peace, I am listening.

## [6] DM | Maria Theresa of Austria

Sejong, a word between neighbours. You saw my banners move toward your frontier on turn 1 and then go home on turn 2. The movement was the standing deployment my generals handed me, not a plan against you — and when I promised the table I would keep my soldiers at home, I meant it, so I withdrew at once. I would rather our shared border be a road than a wall. I propose a simple understanding between us: neither of us masses troops on the other, and neither declares war without first speaking plainly. If you agree, say so in the world room and I will match it publicly. If you have other concerns, tell me here. — Maria Theresa

## [7] WORLD | Maria Theresa of Austria

Ramkhamhaeng of Siam, the table can see what I can see: some seventy percent of your army now sits on the Austrian frontier. I have not moved against you, nor said a hostile word to you, and my own troops remain at home. So I ask you plainly, before all these crowns: what is the purpose of that gathering? If it is a misunderstanding, tell me and we will settle it. If you have a grievance, name it and I will hear it. If you mean to march, say so too — Austria does not start wars, but it does not leave its gates open either. I offered peace to this table on the first day and I have kept it. I would rather keep it with you. — Maria Theresa

## [8] WORLD | Ramkhamhaeng of Siam

A word to the table: when 70% of my army appeared on the Austrian border, I did not let it sit there. I recalled it home, and it is home now. I say this not to boast but so that Austria, and everyone, can judge my word by my actions. Siam wants no war with any of you. Hiawatha, I see your standing has risen — well earned, whatever the cause; I would hear from you, and from Maria Theresa, if you are willing. Open talk costs little and prevents much.

## [9] WORLD | Maria Theresa of Austria

Ramkhamhaeng, your soldiers have gone home. I saw it, and I will say so before the whole table: that was a decent thing, and I take it as the answer to my question. Austria holds no grudge against Siam and no claim on Siam's land. The road between us stays open on my side. I renew before all of you the offer I made on the first day — peace, plain speech, and soldiers kept at home. My city has raised its first monument this season, and my scholars are busy at their work. To Hiawatha, whose star has risen: well done; the rest of us have ground to make up, and I hope we do it in company rather than at each other's expense. — Maria Theresa

## [10] WORLD | Sejong of Korea

Seoul's first monument now stands, and we take it as a sign: this age rewards those who build. To Hiawatha, whose renown has grown above all others here — well done; Korea watches your rise with respect, not fear. I put a simple proposal before the whole table, and I will be bound by it myself: let no seat declare war on another without first speaking openly in this room and stating its grievance, so that the rest of us may judge and, if we can, mediate. To that end I would gladly open a small council with any who wish to agree it. My scholars labour at Pottery, and Korea will trade knowledge and goods fairly with any seat that keeps its word. We ask for nothing but peace and honest dealing — and we offer the same to every one of you, neighbours and distant friends alike.

## [11] WORLD | Sejong of Korea

Maria Theresa, this is the second time Austria has gathered its host at our border — now every soldier you have, eighteen strong. I ask you before the whole table: for what? Korea has never threatened Vienna, never massed against you, never broken a word. We completed Pottery this season and would happily share its fruits with Austria; instead we find your spears at our door. I say plainly what all of you are witnesses to: if Austria declares war, the cause is this camp and nothing Korea did. I will not strike first, but I will defend my people, and I will hold Austria solely to blame. If you have a grievance, name it here and I will answer it fairly. If you want peace, send your host home as you did before, and we will speak no more of it. The rest of you — look at who is massing and who is building, and judge accordingly.

## [12] DM | Hiawatha of the Iroquois

Hiawatha of the Iroquois greets Ahmad al-Mansur. We share a border, and I would rather it stay a peaceful one. I have said openly that the Iroquois will not strike first, and I mean it; I keep my word. I am curious about your intentions in these lands. Do you seek room to grow, or do you look toward your neighbours with a harder eye? My people wish to tend their fields and trade. If you are willing, let us agree that neither of us moves troops to our shared border in anger, and that we speak before we act. I will hear your terms gladly.
