## [1] korea

Korea greets the table. We are a small people in a crowded neighbourhood, and we intend to keep it that way: we claim nothing beyond our own borders and we will not strike first against any seat. Austria and Morocco, you are our neighbours — let us agree plainly that neither side builds up on the other, so that none of us has to guess. To Siam and Iroquois, far from us: we have no quarrel and we wish you well.

We would rather trade words than blows. So a question to each of you: what do you want from this table, and what will you not do? An honest answer costs nothing and buys much. In return, Korea's word: no unprovoked war, no raids, no tricks. If someone proposes a mutual non-aggression pact, we will sign it openly and keep it.

## [2] austria

Greetings from Vienna, Sejong. You have seen my standards move toward our shared frontier, so let me be plain with you rather than let you guess: Austria has no design on Korean land. I would rather our two peoples trade than bleed, and neither of us can afford a border we must guard. I propose a simple understanding between us — no attack, no arming of each other's rivals, and a promise to speak before either of us moves again. If you agree, say so and I will hold to it. If you would rather I pull my men back from the frontier, tell me and I will consider it. I would rather hear your terms than imagine them.

## [3] korea

One day after Korea publicly renounced first strikes and offered a mutual non-aggression pact to all, Austria has moved 80% of its army onto our border. The table can see it. We will not pretend not to.

Maria Theresa: Korea has done you no injury. We ask you plainly, before witnesses — what is this army for, and will you send it home? If it is for us, know that our army sits at home, is stronger than the force you have marched to our door, and will only ever be used to defend our own soil. We will not strike first, but we will not be found asleep either.

Our offer stands, unchanged: mutual withdrawal, and a signed non-aggression pact, in the open where everyone can hold both of us to it. Take it and we go back to being good neighbours. Refuse it and explain yourself to the whole table, not just to me.

Siam, Iroquois, Morocco: I say this in front of you on purpose. Note who offered peace first, and note who answered it with a border camp.

## [4] siam

Greetings from Siam, Hiawatha. We are neighbours and I have no designs on your lands. I notice Austria and Korea have moved armies against one another while you and I have stayed home. I would rather it stay that way between us: a mutual non-aggression understanding, borders quiet, both of us free to grow. Tell me plainly whether that suits you, and I will hold to it.

## [5] austria

To the whole table, so that all of you know where Austria stands. Two turns ago I moved part of my army toward our shared frontier. I did not hide it, and I wrote privately to Sejong of Korea to say plainly that Austria claims no Korean land and wants no war. My offer was simple: no attack, no arming of each other's rivals, and words before movements. I have had no answer. What I have instead is the whole of Korea's army now camped on my border. I will not pretend that is a friendly gesture. So I say it openly, before all of you whose judgment I respect: I renew that offer to Korea, in public this time. Pull your host back, or tell me your terms, and Vienna will match restraint with restraint. But I will not leave my city undefended while an army sits at my door. I would rather be a poor neighbor to no one than a dead one to anyone.

## [6] korea

Maria Theresa, this is Korea, privately. Two armies are now camped on the same border and neither of us has said the word "war." I would like to keep it that way.

I said it in the open and I say it here: I will not attack you. My army moved only after yours did, and it will go home the moment yours does. I am not asking you to lose face — I am offering you a clean way out that we can both call a win.

Here is what I propose: we each pull our armies back to our own cities at the same time, and we sign a non-aggression pact in the open where the whole table can hold us both to it. If there is something you actually want — land, gold, a promise about who I deal with — say it, and I will hear you out honestly rather than guess. A border camp tells me nothing except that you are willing to spend money on tents.

So tell me plainly: what do you want, and what would it take for your army to march home? Answer me honestly and I will answer you the same way.

## [7] siam

Maria Theresa, a word between neighbours. My soldiers are near your border because I am one small state between two larger ones, and I will not be caught unprepared — not because I intend you any harm. Your quarrel is with Korea, not with me, and I have no wish to open a second front for you. I offer a plain mutual non-aggression understanding: our border stays quiet, our armies face elsewhere. If you accept, say so, and I will hold to it as long as you do.

## [8] austria

Ramkhamhaeng, it is Vienna again. Two turns ago I wrote to you asking for a quiet border, no attack, and words before movements. I asked because I meant it, not because I was weak. Your answer, as the whole frontier can now see, is that seven of every ten of your soldiers are camped on my border. I will not pretend to understand why a neighbor I offered peace answers with an army. So I ask you plainly: what do you want? If it is land, know that Vienna will be defended to the last, and that an army in the field bleeds on both sides. If it is security, then here is an offer — I keep my host facing Korea and not you, I pledge no attack on Siam, and I will hear any grievance you care to name. Send your soldiers home and I will regard you as a friend. Leave them there and I must regard you as a threat. The choice is yours, and I would rather it were friendship.

## [9] korea

Ramkhamhaeng, this is Korea, privately. I noticed part of your army has moved away from home and is now facing Austria. I am not asking you to explain yourself to me — I am asking whether we want the same thing.

Here is my situation, honestly: Austria has kept a large force camped on my border for days. I offered a mutual non-aggression pact in the open, and a private off-ramp, and got nothing but more tents. I will not attack first — that is a promise I have made publicly and will keep — but I have no intention of being the one who gets eaten while others watch.

You and I do not share a border, so we have no reason to quarrel. I propose this: you and I agree we will not attack each other, and we agree to talk before either of us does anything drastic about Austria. I am not asking you to fight for me, only to compare notes and not to surprise each other.

Tell me plainly what you want from Austria, and I will tell you what I want: for their army to go home and stay there. If that matches, good. If you want something else, say so — I would rather know now than guess later.

## [10] siam

People of the world, Siam speaks. We are a small nation and we want no war. I have offered my neighbours Austria and Iroquois quiet borders, and I renew that offer openly to everyone at this table: non-aggression between us, open channels, and no army marched at another without cause. Sejong, Maria Theresa — your quarrel is your own, and Siam will not be drawn into it against either side. Any here who want a friend and a fair-dealing partner will find one in me.
